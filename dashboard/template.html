Why some prices are so high

Some contracts pay the hospital a percentage of its list price instead of a fixed fee. When the list price is high (Upstate lists an MRI brain at $8,841), those contracts produce very large dollar amounts. Among contracts that publish a dollar price, they appear only at Upstate and St. Joseph's. The dollar prices that Excellus and MVP publish are fixed fees at all five hospitals, with one exception: MVP's screening mammogram contracts at Upstate.

Not in the comparison: Rochester General

Rochester General's 2026 file lists an insurer dollar price for these outpatient services from one insurer (Aetna, six imaging services) or from none (colonoscopy, endoscopy, outpatient knee replacement). Other insurers appear as formulas. Because a fair comparison is not possible, it is reported as a transparency gap instead.
About the data. Hospital facility prices only (not physician fees), for the base service without billing modifiers. Prices are the negotiated rates each hospital published, not amounts paid on claims or patient out-of-pocket costs. Contracts are counted equally because the files do not say how many people each plan covers. For percent-of-list-price contracts, the dollar figure is the hospital's own estimate. Every cleaning decision is documented in the repository.
